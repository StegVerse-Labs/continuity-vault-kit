# Your First 5 KnowledgeVault Notes

Starting a personal KnowledgeVault can feel overwhelming — so don’t try to organize your whole life at once.

Instead, begin with these **five simple notes**. They create a foundation that future-you (and trusted helpers) will be grateful for.

---

## 1️⃣ About Me — Right Now

**Location:** `01_Notes/`  
**Suggested filename:** `YYYY-MM-DD About Me Now.md`

Write a snapshot of your current life:
- Where you live
- Who you live with
- Your daily routines
- What a “normal week” looks like

This becomes a time capsule of *this version of you*.

---

## 2️⃣ Important People

**Location:** `01_Notes/People/` (create folder if needed)  
**Suggested filename:** `People Overview.md`

List the people who matter most:
- Family
- Close friends
- Key contacts (doctor, lawyer, mentor)

For each person, jot:
- Who they are to you  
- Why they matter  
- How to reach them  

This is one of the most valuable continuity notes you can create.

---

## 3️⃣ Health Overview

**Location:** `03_Records/Health/`  
**Suggested filename:** `Health Overview.md`

Keep this high‑level and practical:
- Ongoing conditions
- Medications or treatments
- Allergies
- Doctors or clinics you use

This can be life‑saving context in the future.

---

## 4️⃣ Life Priorities

**Location:** `01_Notes/`  
**Suggested filename:** `Life Priorities.md`

Answer:
- What matters most to me right now?
- What am I trying to build, protect, or improve?
- What do I *not* want to lose sight of?

This helps future decisions stay aligned with your values.

---

## 5️⃣ Recent Life Events

**Location:** `01_Notes/Timeline/` (create folder if needed)  
**Suggested filename:** `Recent Life Events.md`

Write a short bullet list of important events from the past year or two:
- Moves
- Job changes
- Major trips
- Health events
- Big family milestones

This anchors your timeline and makes future recall much easier.

---

💡 Don’t worry about perfection.  
These notes are meant to grow over time — just start simple.

Your KnowledgeVault becomes powerful not from complexity, but from **consistent, human entries over time**.
